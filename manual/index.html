1What Talanoa is

Talanoa helps a person who can't speak tell you what they want, how they feel, and who they want to see. It was made for the residents of Faleofaz.

It works like a set of talking picture cards on a phone (or a tablet). Every picture says a short sentence out loud in a natural voice, for example the carrot picture says "I want a snack." The words also appear at the top of the screen, so people nearby can read them (tap them to show them big, section 8), and the picture that said them glows for 30 seconds.

There are 19 pages with 216 pictures in total, plus six important buttons that are always on screen.

Four promises the app keeps
  • It never stays silent. If a voice recording can't play, the phone's own voice says it instead.
  • The top row never moves. Its six buttons are always in the same place, so they can be found without looking.
  • It works without internet once it has been set up.
  • Nothing a resident knows moves unless the family asks. New words only fill empty spots, and new pages go at the end, just before ABC, unless the family asks for another spot.

2Setting up a phone

You only do this once per phone (a tablet works the same way). It takes a few minutes and needs Wi-Fi.

  1. 1
    Open Google Chrome

    Use Chrome, not a preview from email or Google Drive.

  2. 2
    Go to the Talanoa address

    Scan the QR code on the front of this guide with the phone's camera, or type tcdeveloper27.github.io/Talanoa (capital T in Talanoa).

  3. 3
    Tap the ⋮ menu (three dots, top right)

    Choose Add to Home screen or Install app, then confirm.

  4. 4
    Open Talanoa from its new icon

    It opens full screen like any other app.

  5. 5
    Leave Talanoa open on Wi-Fi for a few minutes

    It saves the pictures and the voice onto the phone: about 9,300 short recordings with Michael, the voice it starts with. Tapping the board pauses the saving for a moment. Check in Settings: Status says Saving the voice for offline use: … of … while it works (the count goes up by itself while Settings is open), then ✓ Ready to use with no internet.

4Talking with it

Tap a picture once and the phone says the words.

  • 👆
    One tap = one sentence

    No double-tapping needed. A tap still counts if the finger slides a little or rests on the picture for a while.

  • 🔁
    Tap again to repeat

    It starts again from the beginning.

  • ⚡
    Quick taps are fine

    A new tap stops the last sentence and says the new one. Nothing gets stuck.

  • 📳
    A little buzz

    The phone vibrates slightly on each tap, if it can.

  • ✨
    Show someone

    The picture glows for 30 seconds, long enough to lift the phone and show a person what was said.

  • 🎉
    It's fun on purpose

    Every tap makes the picture bounce and throws sparkles: confetti for happy words, hearts for hugs, stars on the Toy Story page, bubbles for drinks. Tapping the same picture a few times in a row sets off a shower. Sad or hurt words get just a few gentle sparkles. It's only decoration: it never gets in the way of a tap, and it waits for the voice to start. It can be turned down or off in Settings, and Calm answers there gives Yes, No, every feeling and the Talk page the same gentle sparkles, so nobody picks an answer just for the show. On a phone set to reduce animations it starts with just bounces and rings.

On the Maverik page, after tapping Drink. The top says "I would like a drink", and Drink glows.

5Moving between pages

Use the big ◀ ▶ arrows to flip through the pages, like turning the pages of a book, or swipe sideways across the pictures.

  • 👈
    Swipe to turn the page

    Slide a finger sideways across the pictures, like on a phone's home screen. The page follows the finger; let go early and it springs back. A swipe never says anything.

  • ◀▶
    Or tap the arrows

    Each arrow shows a small picture of the page it goes to. After the last page it goes back to the first.

  • 🗂️
    Jump straight to a page

    Tap the page name in the middle. All the pages appear as big buttons (scroll down for the rest): tap one to go there. To close the list without choosing, scroll to ✕ Close at the end, or press Back.

Pages turning by accident?Swiping can be turned off in Settings. The arrows always work.

6The Mom & Dad page

All the parent and grandparent tiles are together on one page.

Every Dad tile has the matching Mom tile directly underneath it: Dad and Mom, Call Dad and Call Mom, Dad's house and Mom's house, Dad coming? and Mom coming? The bottom row has Grandma, Grandpa, Nana and Papa.

This layout is deliberateThe family asked that Mom always appears under Dad. The app is built so this can't be changed by accident.
When a resident taps "Call Dad" or "Call Mom"Treat it as a real request: tell them what will happen ("Let's call Mom after lunch") so they know they were heard.

7The Talk page

Words for saying what they think, not just what they want.

Most of what people say isn't asking for things. It's commenting ("That's funny!"), giving an opinion ("I don't like it"), and steering the conversation ("Wait", "Your turn"). The Talk page gives residents those words too.

8Show it big · About me

For a cashier, a noisy room, someone across the table, or someone new.

  • 🔎
    Tap the words at the top

    While they're still showing (30 seconds), the last thing said fills the screen in giant letters: hold the phone up, or hand it over. After that, tap the picture again first. 🔊 Say it again repeats it. ✕ Close (or Back) goes back to the board, and it closes by itself after a minute.

  • 👋
    Hi, I'm Brenton (top row: about me)

    Says: "Hi, I'm Brenton. I can't talk with my mouth, so I talk with this phone. Please be patient and give me time. My emergency contacts are in this phone's Emergency information." Tap the words at the top to show them big. Use it with anyone new: a driver, a cashier, a doctor, a police officer or a paramedic. The People page has a short Hi, I'm Brenton for a quick hello.

Opening by accident?Settings → Reading → untick Tap the words to show them big. Then the about-me message can't be shown big either, so tick it again before an outing.

Set up the phone's Emergency information

The about-me message points to the phone's own Emergency information. Paramedics and police know to look there, and they can see it from the lock screen without unlocking the phone. Phone numbers and medical details belong there, not in Talanoa, because the Talanoa website is public.

  1. 1
    Open the phone's Settings app

    The phone's own settings (the gear icon among the apps), not Talanoa's ⚙.

  2. 2
    Search for "Emergency"

    Open Emergency information. On some phones it's called Medical info, or it's inside Safety & emergency or the Personal Safety app.

  3. 3
    Add medical details and emergency contacts

    Conditions, allergies, medicines, and who to call (family, and Faleofaz).

  4. 4
    Turn on "Show on lock screen"

    To check it: lock the phone, swipe up, tap Emergency, then Emergency information.

10The ABC page

The alphabet, for spelling anything that isn't on a picture.

  • 🔤
    Tap letters

    Each letter says its name. Space goes between words, ⌫ takes back one letter, Clear starts again.

  • 🔊
    Speak

    Says the whole word or sentence, and puts it at the top, where it can be shown big.

  • 🗣️
    The same voice as the pictures

    The letters and every word on the board are recorded in every voice, and Michael (the voice it starts with) also knows about 9,000 everyday words. A word the chosen voice doesn't have (often a name) is spelled out, letter by letter, in that same voice. With Built-in voice, the phone's own voice reads it all.

Not spelling yet? That's fineLet them explore the letters, and read back what they type. Playing with letters is how spelling starts.

Two extras can be switched on in Settings: word suggestions above the letters, and a row of numbers (section 12).

11The Body and Sick pages

For saying where it hurts, how much, and how they feel when they're unwell. They come right after Ouch (pages 4 and 5), so all three health pages are together. Head, Tummy, Tooth, Ear, Eyes, Arm, Leg and Foot are on the Ouch page.

  • 1
    Take every tap seriously

    Treat it as real until you've checked on them, even if they sometimes tap for fun.

  • 2
    Find the spot

    Point and ask: "Does it hurt here?" They can answer with Yes and No on the top row.

  • 3
    Ask how bad, and since when

    A little, A lot or Really bad are on the Body page; Just now and Yesterday are on the Sick page.

  • 4
    Write it down

    What they said and when, for the nurse or doctor. Better ("I feel better now") tells you when it has passed.

12Sentences, suggestions and more

Things other talking apps have, added in October 2026. The Words page, Find a word and This week are always there. The other five are switched on in Settings (section 13) and are off until someone ticks them, so the board stays exactly as he knows it.

  • The Words page (just before ABC): 12 single words, I, You, Want, Like, Go, Get, Do, Look, More, Not, Different, Finished. Each says just its word. On their own they answer quickly ("Different!"); with Build sentences they combine into things nobody programmed, like I · Want · Go · Outside.
  • Build sentences (Settings → Talking in sentences): every picture is still said straight away, and its words also line up at the top. 🔊 on the left says the whole sentence, one picture after another; ✕ starts again. The sentence stays until ✕ (after 12 pictures the oldest drops off), and tapping the words at the top shows all of it big. Words spoken on the ABC page join the sentence too.
  • Word suggestions on the ABC page: three words above the letters, finishing the word being typed. Words he has spoken before come first (they're remembered while Count taps on this phone is on, and Clear the counts forgets them). Tap one to finish the word (it's said, and a space follows).
  • Numbers on the ABC page: a row of numbers under the letters, 1 to 9 then 0, like a phone keypad. Numbers are said as words: 15 is "fifteen", 2026 "two thousand twenty six".
  • Recent sentences with the big words: under the big words, the last few things said; tap one to show it and say it again (handy when someone missed it).
  • Bigger pictures (6 on a screen) (Settings → Screen): for someone who needs bigger targets. Each page becomes two screens, "Food" and "Food 2", in the same order.
  • Find a word (in Settings, under Pages to show): type part of a word, like pizz; it lists each picture that matches and its page (Pizza Food page). Tap a result and the board turns to that page and outlines the picture (nothing is said).
  • This week (Settings → Most-used pictures): taps on each of the last 7 days, and pictures used for the first time this week. Like the other counts, it stays on this phone and is never sent anywhere.
Try it on a staff phone firstSwitch an option on on your own phone and show him: point to I, Want, Go… as you say them. Then decide together, with the family, whether it goes on his phone.

15Without Wi-Fi

Once the phone shows ✓ Ready to use with no internet, Talanoa works anywhere: on outings, in the car, at the store, or when the Wi-Fi is down. Pictures, words and the chosen voice are all saved on the phone.

After choosing a new voiceKeep the phone on Wi-Fi until Settings → Status says ✓ Ready to use with no internet: about a minute for most voices, several minutes for Michael (it also saves the ABC page's 9,000 words). Until then, a sentence that isn't saved yet plays from the internet, and with no internet the phone's own voice says it. It's never silent.

16Helping someone talk

The most helpful thing staff can do costs nothing: use the board yourself, every day.

  1. 🗣️
    Use the board when you talk to them

    As you speak, tap the main word on their board: "Let's go to Maverik", "Do you want more?" Children hear thousands of hours of talk before they say a word; someone learning a talker needs to see it used the same way. Ask first ("Can I show you on your phone?"), or point to the paper board instead.

  2. 🚫
    Show, don't test

    No quizzing ("Show me Drink!"). Just use the words naturally while you talk.

  3. ⏳
    Wait

    After a question, count to ten in your head before helping. Finding the right page and picture takes time.

  4. 💬
    Answer every message, even when the answer is no

    "I hear you, you want a drink. We'll get one after lunch." That's how people learn their words work.

  5. 🙅
    No means no

    "No", "Stop", "Not now" and "Leave me alone" are real. Respect them whenever you safely can, or explain why not.

  6. 🎈
    Playing with it is learning

    Tapping lots of pictures, or the same one again and again, is how people explore their words, the way a baby babbles. Don't stop it: say the words back, and join in.

  7. 📍
    It's their voice: keep it with them

    All day, charged, in every room. Never take the board away as a punishment or to get someone to behave.

  8. 📈
    Notice what's missing

    Settings → Most-used pictures shows which words get used (your own taps, when you show words, are counted too). When someone seems to want a word that isn't there, tell the family.

Everyone has the right to communicateThe Communication Bill of Rights (ASHA, 3rd edition, 2024) lists twelve rights. They include: to be treated with dignity and respect; to communicate in a way that fits their culture and language; to be spoken to directly, not talked about; to get a response to every message, even when the answer is no; to ask for things and to refuse them; to share feelings, comments and opinions; to make real choices; and to have a working talker with them at all times. asha.org/njc/communication-bill-of-rights
Free trainingProject Core has 12 short, free training modules on teaching with a talker. They're made for schools, but the ideas work at any age.
Talanoa supports, it doesn't replaceTalanoa is a simple, friendly board, not a full professional speech system. A speech-language pathologist who specializes in AAC can suggest what each resident needs.

No sound at allTurn the volume up with the phone's side buttons. Check the phone isn't on silent. In Settings, check Volume isn't low.
The voice sounds roboticIn Settings, Built-in voice is probably chosen. Choose Michael (or another named voice). If a named voice is already chosen, the phone's own voice is filling in because that voice isn't saved on the phone yet: connect to Wi-Fi and wait for ✓ Ready to use with no internet (section 15).
Only a brown bar, no picturesIt was opened in a preview (from email or Google Drive). Open Talanoa from its home-screen icon, or in Chrome.
A page is missingSettings → Pages to show → tick it.
Settings won't openPress and hold the ⚙ for a full second. A quick tap does nothing, on purpose.
New words from the family aren't showingConnect to Wi-Fi, then Settings → Check for updates now.
"404 / There isn't a GitHub Pages site here"The address was typed wrong. Use the QR code, or type tcdeveloper27.github.io/Talanoa with a capital T.
The ▶ arrow (or a swipe) jumped back to "I want"That's normal: after the last page it starts again at the first.
Pages keep turning by accidentSettings → untick Swipe to change pages. The ◀ ▶ arrows still work.
The words keep adding up at the top, with 🔊 and ✕That's Build sentences (section 12): each picture's words line up so 🔊 can say them all. ✕ clears them. To turn it off: Settings → Talking in sentences → untick Build sentences.
Only 6 pictures on a screen, and pages called "Food 2"That's Bigger pictures (section 12). To go back to 12: Settings → Screen → untick Bigger pictures.
A picture is glowingThat's the last picture tapped. It stops glowing by itself after 30 seconds, or as soon as another picture is tapped.
Too many sparkles, or the phone feels slowSettings → Fun effects → A little or Off.
The words suddenly filled the whole screenThat's Show it big (section 8). Tap ✕ Close or Back; it also closes by itself after a minute. To stop it opening by accident: Settings → Reading → untick Tap the words to show them big.
"The microphone didn't start" when recordingClose any call or other app using the microphone. Then in Chrome: ⋮ → Settings → Site settings → Microphone, and allow tcdeveloper27.github.io. Try again.
A photo or recording has disappearedThey're kept on the phone only. Hold ⚙ → Restore a backup and pick the backup file.
The Tongan words don't sound rightThe computer voice can't speak Tongan. Record them in a real voice (section 9).
The ABC page spells a word out instead of saying itWith Michael (the voice it starts with) it says the words on the board and about 9,000 everyday words; the other voices know the board's words and the number words. Any other word (often a name) is spelled out in the same voice. To have a word added, tell Tim (section 20).
The bottom row is hidden behind the phone's ◀ ● ■ buttonsClose Talanoa and open it again (it fits itself to the phone the first time). If it's still hidden: Settings → Screen → tick Bottom row hidden behind the ◀ ● ■ buttons?
The screen keeps turning offSee "Screen timeout" in section 19.
Something else looks wrongClose Talanoa completely and open it again from its icon. If it still happens, take a photo of the screen and email it to Tim Broussard at tcdeveloper@gmail.com.

19Looking after the phone

  • 🔋
    Charge every night

    A flat battery means a resident with no voice.

  • ⏲️
    Screen timeout

    On most Android phones: Settings → Display → Screen timeout, and choose a longer time (for example 10 minutes).

  • 🔊
    Volume

    Use the side buttons while Talanoa is talking to set how loud it is.

  • 🧽
    Clean and protect

    Wipe the screen with a slightly damp cloth. A sturdy case is a good idea.

  • 🔈
    Loud places

    A small clip-on Bluetooth speaker makes the voice easier to hear in shops and cars (Bluetooth adds a short delay before each sentence). Show it big helps too (section 8).

  • 🆘
    Emergency information

    Set it up once, so first responders can see it on the lock screen (section 8).

20Asking for changes

New words, different wording, a real photo of someone instead of a picture: the family can add these. Write down what's needed (for example "add Swing, 'I want to go on the swing'") and pass it to Tim Broussard. The change reaches the phone automatically, usually the same day. Settings → Most-used pictures can help decide what to add. A photo of a person is best added on the phone itself (section 9), so it stays private: anything Tim adds goes on the public website.
